Zalor

Building an AI startup from scratch — shipping a v1 MCP platform, running 45+ customer discovery conversations, and pivoting to AI reliability testing based on what we heard.

Timeline

Role
Co-founder & CEO
Competitors

Where We Started

Zalor started as a platform for managing MCP servers — making it easy for teams to connect AI agents to any REST API without writing tool definitions by hand. We shipped a v1 with Weav as our first design partner and started talking to potential users.

The product worked. But the conversations kept surfacing a different, more urgent problem.

What Customer Discovery Revealed

Across 45+ conversations — sourced through warm leads, LinkedIn, Reddit, and Hacker News outreach — the signal was consistent: teams shipping AI agents weren't confident their agents would behave correctly in production. Static test suites couldn't catch the behavioral failures that actually mattered.

"We have evals, but they test outputs, not behavior. We have no idea if the agent would handle a confused or adversarial user."

The MCP platform solved a real problem, but it was an infrastructure problem. The behavioral reliability problem was closer to the point of pain — and nobody had solved it well.

The Pivot

Within weeks of shipping v1, we pivoted. The new direction: AI reliability testing through persona simulation. Instead of static evals that check outputs, Zalor simulates how different types of users — confused, adversarial, edge-case — interact with an AI agent, and surfaces behavioral failures before they reach production.

The positioning decision was deliberate. Patronus AI and HoneyHive both operate in the eval space, but their approach is output-centric: did the model return the right answer? Zalor's bet is that behavioral simulation — modeling how users actually interact — catches a class of failures that output evals miss entirely.

Behavioral simulation vs. static evals: the distinction is whether you're testing what the agent says or how it behaves across a realistic distribution of users.

What I Own

All of product, GTM, and engineering alongside my co-founder. That means defining the roadmap, running customer discovery, positioning against competitors, and building the product. At this stage, the founder is the PM.

The most valuable thing I've done isn't any individual feature — it's keeping the scope tight enough that we can move fast while staying close enough to users to know when we're wrong.

What's Next

The core product question is: what does a persona library look like? Users need a starting point — a set of pre-built personas (confused first-time user, power user pushing limits, adversarial user trying to jailbreak) that they can run against their agent immediately, then customize.

The distribution question is equally important. The buyers are AI engineering teams at companies actively shipping agents to production — not teams evaluating whether to adopt AI. Outreach through Hacker News, AI-focused communities, and warm intros from early users at Zoom has been the most efficient channel so far.
